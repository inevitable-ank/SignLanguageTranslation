Language is a means of communication, and in this digital age, communication is the key to a plethora of possibilities. Humans may study, write, and talk in a variety of languages, but communicating in a digital world remains difficult for the deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) community. Most non-deaf and non-DHH persons are unfamiliar with sign language, a crucial means of communication. To address this communication barrier between DHH and non-DHH persons, we looked at existing solutions and how we could create a better platform with maximum accessibility and future potential.
Sign Language is not not just hand gestures, it is a combination of facial expressions and body movements. It is a non verbal language and vary depending on the country and can also differ from area to area and have their own vocabularies and grammar. We developed our project idea around American sign language, which is the worldwide accepted standard.
The goal of this project is to examine the research articles and learn how certain people developed their answers. Sign language is a form of gesture and visual communication used by people with congenital DHH and acquired DHH. According to World Federation of the Deaf figures, there are 70 million DHH people worldwide, with over 200 sign languages. Thus, by enhancing translation technology, we can bridge the communication gap between DHH and non-DHH individuals.

The project can be categorized into three parts, Improving the accuracy of SLR. Adding text to speech for real-time translation, and testing the model on video conferencing. With the addition of video, it can be included in any application to pass information and provide hearing impaired people with easy access to knowledge.
